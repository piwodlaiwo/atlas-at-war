Atlas at War

Why "Atlas"?

"Atlas" is derived from ancient Greek, Ἄτλας (Átlas), and was the name of the Titan who was condemned to be "The Bearer (of the Heavens)" after his failed attempt at taking on the Olympian gods in battle for control of the universe.

The word became associated with cartography in the 16th century when Gerardus Mercator published his world atlas, the Atlas of Mercator. A map is a map; a book of maps is an atlas — which is why this game is built to be waged across more than one.

The territories

Before the war began, some neighbouring lands had already been unified — so Spain and Portugal march as one (Iberia), Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania as the Baltics, Norway and Sweden as Scandinavia, and so on. Each coloured bloc on the map is one such territory.

How to play

  • Reinforce — each turn you get armies based on how much you hold (~1 per 3 lands, min 3) plus a bonus for controlling a whole region. Click your lands to place them.
  • Attack — click one of your lands, then an adjacent enemy. Both roll dice; more armies means better odds, but never a guarantee.
  • Move armies — once per turn, march a stack across your own connected lands.
  • Supply lines — strand a single enemy land away from their main force and it's cut off (⚠): it can't reinforce or attack and loses a unit each turn.
  • Bonus missions — a hidden objective for extra armies (optional).
  • World events — fate intervenes at the start of each round.
  • Win by conquering the entire map.

Geography, borders, and regions come live from the MapJSON API.
