Atlas* at War

How to play

  • Reinforce — each turn you get armies based on how much you hold (~1 per 3 lands, min 3) plus a bonus for controlling a whole region. Click your lands to place them.
  • Attack — click one of your lands, then an adjacent enemy. Both roll dice; more armies means better odds, but never a guarantee.
  • Move armies — once per turn, march a stack across your own connected lands.
  • Supply lines — strand a single enemy land away from their main force and it's cut off (⚠): it can't reinforce or attack and loses a unit each turn.
  • Bonus missions — a hidden objective for extra armies (optional).
  • World events — fate intervenes at the start of each round.
  • Win by conquering the entire map.

Before the war

Long before the first army marched, the map had already been redrawn by smaller quarrels. Spain and Portugal, weary of squabbling over one peninsula, shook hands and became Iberia. Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania — three small siblings on a cold shore — swore a pact and stood as one, the Baltics. Norway, restless and hungry, fell upon Sweden in the night, bent it to its will, and raised a single banner over both, which it named Scandinavia. Belgium and the Netherlands pooled their lowlands; the Czechs and Slovaks clasped hands as Czechoslovakia.

And so, when the great war finally came, it was fought not by nations but by these blocs — each coloured territory a marriage or a conquest already settled.

* "Atlas" is derived from ancient Greek, Ἄτλας (Átlas), the name of the Titan condemned to be "The Bearer (of the Heavens)" after his failed attempt at taking on the Olympian gods in battle for control of the universe. The word became associated with cartography in the 16th century, when Gerardus Mercator published his world atlas, the Atlas of Mercator.
