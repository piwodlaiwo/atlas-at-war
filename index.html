Atlas at War

How to play

  • Attack — click one of your lands, then an adjacent enemy. Both roll dice; more armies means better odds, but never a guarantee.
  • Reinforce — each turn you get armies based on how much you hold (~1 per 3 lands, min 3) plus a bonus for controlling a whole region.
  • Move armies — once per turn, march a stack across your own connected lands.
  • Supply lines — strand a single enemy land away from their main force and it's cut off (⚠): it can't reinforce or attack and loses a unit each turn.
  • Bonus missions — a hidden objective for extra armies (optional).
  • World events — fate intervenes at the start of each round.
  • Win by conquering the entire map.

Before the war

Long before the first army marched, each map had already been redrawn by smaller quarrels.

In Europe, Spain and Portugal shook hands and became Iberia; Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania swore a pact as the Baltics; and Norway, restless and hungry, fell upon Sweden in the night, bent it to its will, and raised one banner over both — Scandinavia.

In Africa, the desert cousins Mali and Mauritania roamed as one; Morocco swallowed the Western Sahara; the Horn — Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti and Somalia — bound their fates on the Red Sea; and along the great river, Zambia, Malawi, Zimbabwe and Mozambique flowed together as the Zambezi.

In the Americas, Guatemala gathered Belize and El Salvador beneath one roof; Peru and Ecuador split the Andes between friends; Argentina drew Uruguay into its embrace; and the three Guianas kept watch as one over the northern shore.

And so, when the great war finally came, it was fought not by nations but by these blocs — each coloured territory a marriage or a conquest already settled.

Atlas — from ancient Greek Ἄτλας (Átlas), the Titan condemned to be "The Bearer (of the Heavens)" after his failed attempt at taking on the Olympian gods for control of the universe. The word became associated with maps in the 16th century, when Gerardus Mercator published his world atlas, the Atlas of Mercator.
